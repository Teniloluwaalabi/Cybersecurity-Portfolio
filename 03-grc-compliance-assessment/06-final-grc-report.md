# Final GRC & Compliance Assessment Report

## 1. Executive Summary

This report presents the findings of a simulated Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) assessment for FinTrust Financial Services Ltd., a fictional fintech company.

The assessment reviewed selected security controls, mapped them to relevant areas of the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) and ISO/IEC 27001, identified control gaps, and proposed remediation actions.

The assessment identified **10 security and governance gaps: 1 Critical, 7 High, and 2 Medium**. The most urgent concern is inconsistent multi-factor authentication (MFA), followed by gaps in access reviews, security awareness, patch management, monitoring, vendor risk management, data protection validation, backup testing, governance, and incident-response exercises.

These findings represent hypothetical conditions created for this portfolio exercise. They are not results of a real audit or an assessment of an actual company's environment.

## 2. Assessment Objectives

The objectives were to:

- Define the scope of a simulated GRC assessment.
- Evaluate selected security control areas.
- Map controls to relevant NIST CSF functions and ISO/IEC 27001 topics.
- Identify control gaps and assess their relative risk.
- Recommend practical remediation actions and target timelines.
- Demonstrate risk-based security governance and compliance documentation.

## 3. Scope and Methodology

The assessment focused on selected control areas relevant to a fintech organization, including identity and access management, employee awareness, vulnerability management, security monitoring, third-party risk, data protection, backup and recovery, security governance, and incident response.

The methodology consisted of:

1. Defining the fictional organization's assessment scope.
2. Reviewing the selected control areas against documented assessment criteria.
3. Mapping control areas to relevant framework functions and topics.
4. Recording identified gaps and assigning relative severity.
5. Developing a remediation plan with proposed owners, priorities, and target dates.

No real systems were accessed, no technical testing was performed, and no independent evidence was collected from an actual organization. The results are therefore illustrative rather than independently verified.

## 4. Framework Alignment

The assessment used the NIST Cybersecurity Framework functions—Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover—as an organizing structure.

Selected control topics were also mapped to relevant areas of ISO/IEC 27001, including access control, awareness, vulnerability management, supplier security, incident management, and business continuity.

These mappings indicate potential alignment areas only. They do not establish conformity with either framework, and they should not be interpreted as certification or a formal compliance determination.

## 6. Recommended Remediation

### Immediate priority — within 7 days

- **MFA:** Enforce MFA for privileged accounts and other high-risk access, then verify implementation.
- Confirm that exceptions are documented, approved, and time-limited.

### High-priority improvements — within 30 days

- Introduce periodic user-access reviews and documented approval of access changes.
- Deliver security awareness training and track completion.
- Establish a documented vulnerability and patch-management process.
- Improve logging, alerting, and escalation for suspicious activity.
- Establish a vendor risk assessment and review process.
- Validate relevant data protection controls and document evidence.
- Test backup restoration and document recovery results.

### Governance and continuous improvement — within 60–90 days

- Assign policy owners and establish a review schedule.
- Conduct an incident-response tabletop exercise.
- Record lessons learned, assign corrective actions, and track closure.

The proposed deadlines are planning targets for this exercise, not universal regulatory requirements. Management should validate them against risk, resources, and operational constraints.

## 7. Monitoring and Reporting

Progress should be tracked through a remediation register containing:

- Finding ID and description
- Severity and business impact
- Assigned owner
- Agreed target date
- Remediation status
- Evidence required for closure
- Residual risk and any approved exception

Suggested measures include MFA coverage, completion of access reviews, employee training completion, overdue critical patches, monitoring coverage, vendor assessments completed, successful backup restoration tests, and incident-response exercise actions closed.

A finding should only be marked resolved when appropriate evidence has been reviewed. Any remaining risk should be documented and escalated to an authorized risk owner for a decision.

## 8. Conclusion

This simulated assessment demonstrates how a risk-based GRC process can organize security gaps, connect control objectives to recognized frameworks, prioritize corrective actions, and support management oversight.

The most urgent recommendation is to address inconsistent MFA, followed by the High-priority control gaps. Governance improvements and incident-response exercises should strengthen the organization's longer-term security management process.

In a real engagement, the next step would be to validate these findings through stakeholder interviews, policy and evidence review, and authorized technical testing where appropriate.

## 9. Limitations and Disclaimer

FinTrust Financial Services Ltd. is fictional. This project is an educational portfolio exercise intended to demonstrate GRC documentation and analytical skills.

The assessment is not a formal audit, legal opinion, certification, or verified statement of compliance. Framework mappings and findings are illustrative and would require evidence-based validation in a real organization.
