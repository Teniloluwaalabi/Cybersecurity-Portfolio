# GRC Remediation Plan – FinTrust Financial Services Ltd.

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes a prioritized remediation plan for the gaps identified during the fictional GRC assessment.

It assigns recommended actions, responsible roles, target completion periods, and success measures to help FinTrust improve its information-security controls.

> **Disclaimer:** This is a fictional portfolio exercise. The owners and target periods are proposed planning assumptions, not actual organizational commitments.

## 2. Remediation Priorities

Remediation priorities are based on the potential impact of each gap, the importance of the affected control, and the urgency of reducing the associated risk.

- **P1 – Critical:** Immediate attention; target within 7 days.
- **P2 – High:** Prioritized action; target within 30 days.
- **P3 – Medium:** Planned improvement; target within 60–90 days.

These are proposed portfolio targets, not universal regulatory deadlines.

## 4. Implementation Approach

### Phase 1: Immediate Risk Reduction

**Target: Within 7 days**

- Enforce MFA on corporate and high-risk accounts.
- Review any documented MFA exceptions.
- Assign owners to all critical remediation actions.
- Confirm that urgent security issues have appropriate escalation.

**Expected outcome:** Reduced exposure to credential-based account compromise.

### Phase 2: Priority Control Improvements

**Target: Within 30 days**

- Review access permissions.
- Conduct security awareness training.
- Establish patch-management tracking.
- Improve security-log monitoring.
- Begin risk-based vendor assessments.
- Validate data-protection controls.
- Test critical backups.

**Expected outcome:** Improved consistency and oversight across key security controls.

### Phase 3: Governance and Continuous Improvement

**Target: Within 60–90 days**

- Formalize security policy ownership.
- Establish periodic governance reviews.
- Conduct an incident-response tabletop exercise.
- Record lessons learned and outstanding actions.
- Review progress with management.

**Expected outcome:** Stronger security governance and a repeatable improvement process.

## 5. Remediation Tracking

Each action should be tracked using the following fields:

| Tracking Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Remediation ID | Unique reference for the action |
| Action Owner | Person or team responsible |
| Target Date | Agreed completion deadline |
| Current Status | Not Started, In Progress, Blocked, or Completed |
| Evidence | Documentation demonstrating completion |
| Residual Risk | Risk remaining after remediation |
| Reviewer | Person responsible for validating the result |

An action should not be marked completed solely because a task was reported as finished. The assigned reviewer should check the supporting evidence.

## 6. Success Measures

The following proposed measures can help management evaluate progress.

| Measure | Desired Outcome |
|---|---|
| MFA coverage | All in-scope accounts protected, with approved exceptions documented |
| Access reviews | Reviews completed and unnecessary access removed |
| Security awareness | Training completion tracked and phishing reports reviewed |
| Patch management | Vulnerabilities tracked and remediated according to defined priorities |
| Security monitoring | Priority logs available and assigned alerts reviewed |
| Vendor risk | High-risk vendors assessed and findings tracked |
| Backup recovery | Critical recovery tests completed and results documented |
| Policy governance | Policies have assigned owners and review dates |
| Incident response | Tabletop exercise completed and improvement actions recorded |

These are proposed measures. Baselines and actual performance results would need to be collected from the organization.

## 7. Risk Acceptance and Escalation

If a remediation action cannot be completed by its target date, the owner should document:

- The reason for the delay
- The potential business impact
- Existing compensating controls
- The revised target date
- The person authorized to approve any risk acceptance

Critical or high-risk issues should be escalated to the appropriate management or risk owner.

Risk acceptance should be formally documented and reviewed periodically.

## 8. Reporting and Oversight

The GRC function should maintain a remediation register and provide periodic updates to management.

Reports should summarize:

- Open critical and high-priority actions
- Overdue remediation items
- Actions completed and independently verified
- Risks accepted by authorized decision-makers
- Recurring issues
- Areas requiring additional resources

## 9. Conclusion

The remediation plan translates the identified GRC gaps into practical, trackable actions.

Its effectiveness depends on clear ownership, realistic deadlines, supporting evidence, management oversight, and periodic review of remaining risk.
